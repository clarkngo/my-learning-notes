# 🤖 Six Levels for Integrating Generative AI into Programming Courses

Mahon, J., Mac Namee, B., & Becker, B. A. (2024). Guidelines for the evolving role of generative ai in introductory programming based on emerging practice. *In Proceedings of the 2024 on Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education V. 1* (pp. 10-16). [https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3649217.3653602](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3649217.3653602)

The provided table outlines **six levels** (A through F) that educators can consider when integrating Generative AI (GenAI) into **Introductory Programming Courses**, along with specific **actions** and their **descriptions** for each level.

| Level | Focus | Actions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level A** | **GenAI Avoidance** | **Ignoring GenAI:** Not discussing or acknowledging its existence.<br> **Banning GenAI:** Prohibiting any use of GenAI in the classroom or for assignments.<br> **Awareness Only:** Briefly mentioning GenAI without any in-depth exploration. |
| **Level B** | **Hands Off GenAI Interaction** | **Ethical Discussions:** Focusing on the ethical implications, potential misuse, and societal impacts of GenAI. <br>**Case Studies:** Analyzing real-world GenAI examples, without hands-on experience. <br>**Comparative Analysis:** Comparing GenAI outputs with human outputs to discern differences. <br>**Guest Lectures:** Inviting experts to discuss the technology without hands-on sessions. <br>**Demonstrations:** Showing students pre-made demonstrations without allowing them to interact. |
| **Level C** | **Guided Interaction with GenAI** | **Interactive Demos:** Allowing students to interact with pre-trained models on platforms like OpenAI. <br>**Guided Tutorials:** Providing step-by-step tutorials for students to follow, without independent projects. <br>**Tool Exploration:** Introducing students to platforms and tools that utilize GenAI, such as chatbots. <br>**Reading Assignments:** Assigning research papers or articles about GenAI for students to review. <br>**Research Projects:** Encouraging students to research specific aspects of GenAI. <br>**Quizzes and Tests:** Testing students' knowledge about GenAI concepts. <br>**Critique Sessions:** Analyzing and critiquing the outputs of GenAI models. |
| **Level D** | **Independent GenAI Projects** | **Simple Projects:** Assigning basic projects where students use GenAI tools under guidance. <br>**Advanced Projects:** Assigning more complex projects where students have more freedom in their GenAI use. <br>**Hackathons:** Organizing events where students compete to create innovative solutions using GenAI. <br>**Integration in Other Subjects:** Using GenAI as a tool in subjects like art, literature, or history. |
| **Level E** | **GenAI Integration** | **Feedback Mechanisms:** Using GenAI to provide feedback on students' work or assignments. <br>**GenAI Collaboration:** Students use GenAI as a sparring partner on tasks. <br>**Peer Teaching:** Encouraging knowledgeable students to teach peers about GenAI. <br>**External Collaborations:** Partnering with companies or researchers for students to gain real-world experience with GenAI. <br>**Internships:** Facilitating internships focused on GenAI research or applications. <br>**Open-ended Exploration:** Allowing students to explore and experiment with GenAI without strict guidelines. <br>**Curriculum Integration:** Incorporating Generative AI as a regular part of the curriculum, not just a one-off topic. <br>**Student-led Initiatives/Community Projects:** Encouraging students to start clubs or groups focused on GenAI, or using GenAI to solve community or societal problems. |
| **Level F** | **Embedded GenAI** | **Full Embrace:** Making GenAI a central theme of the course. <br>**Certification:** Offering specialized courses leading to certification in GenAI. |
