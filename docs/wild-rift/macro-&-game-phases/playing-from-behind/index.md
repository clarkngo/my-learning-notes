# How To Win When Behind Thousands Of Gold
This section is extracted from [talanwr](https://www.youtube.com/@talanwr)'s YouTube video: [How To Win When Behind Thousands Of Gold](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P1GMb8FeyQo) 

## General Philosophy and Risk Management
Playing from behind requires a high level of risk management. Since you are likely to lose the game when behind by a significant amount (e.g., 8k or 10k gold), it is acceptable to take riskier plays, such as flipping random Barons or Elders, or forcing a stun onto an enemy carry.

You must choose moments to come back, which usually involve finding times when the enemy has overextended or hasn't recalled. You should be willing to "hard overforce a fight" because there is little to lose, and this represents the best time to mount a comeback.

## Leveraging Numbers Advantage and Cross-Mapping
The fundamental approach to playing from behind is to beat the enemy with a numbers advantage rather than through raw gold or strength (wallet).
1. Mismatching Numbers: The primary goal is constantly mismatching numbers across the map. This might involve creating a 3v4 on one side and a 1v2 elsewhere, or a 4v3 somewhere else.
2. Opposite Side Plays: When the enemy team is forcing an objective (like Dragon or Baron), you should generally be focused on making plays on the opposite side of the map. This strategy involves getting gold elsewhere, such as securing a turret, farming waves, or finding an isolated kill. This is how you accumulate gold to get strong enough for later, more important objectives (like Baron or Elder).
3. Using Numbers Advantage After A Win: If you win an early fight (even when down 10k gold), immediately use the resulting numbers advantage to push out waves, force fights onto remaining fed enemies (to secure more kills), or simply to force out enemy cooldowns (like ultimate or flash) for future objectives.

## Capitalizing on Enemy Mistakes and Carries
You rely heavily on the enemy making mistakes, particularly those that create a temporary numbers advantage or negate the impact of their most fed players.
1. Targeting Carries: If the enemy has several highly fed champions (carries, like Le/Lemonth or Darius), you must ensure they die in a fight. A common strategy is to force a fight when the enemy carries are known to be somewhere else on the map (e.g., a side lane).
2. Punishing Overextensions: Capitalize on instances where the enemy wins a fight but doesn't recall, tries to take more than they should, and therefore lacks crucial resources (ults, flashes, or spent gold). The goal is to stall these overextending players until your teammates respawn.
3. Seizing the Moment (The Throw): All preparation leads to the moment when the enemy makes one or two critical errors, often "throwing" around key objectives. This is the time to seize the advantage. The most opportune moment to force a fight is when an enemy carry gets caught and dies. This changes a losing 5v5 into a winnable 5v4.

## Objective Strategy
Objective management depends heavily on the game state and enemy positioning:
- Avoid Useless Objectives: When significantly behind (e.g., 8k gold), you should generally avoid forcing fights over "useless objectives" like random dragons. Instead, retreat and get gold elsewhere to strengthen your position.
- Forcing Fights Around Objectives: When an enemy carry dies, the purpose of contesting an objective shifts from securing the objective itself to forcing a fight before that carry respawns. The objective is a common gathering point, making it easy to force the engagement.
- Flipping Objectives: When behind, it is "totally fine" to attempt to steal or "flip" major objectives (Baron or Elder) even if the enemy jungler is alive. This is a 50/50 risk because successfully getting the objective likely wins the game, and losing the flip results in the loss you were already facing. Conversely, you should never randomly flip objectives when you are ahead, as natural game progression will likely lead to victory.
• Timing with Baron/Dragon: One of the best times to force a random fight is when Baron and Dragon spawn at a similar time, as the enemy often gets distracted by overforcing the Dragon.
## Win Conditions, Itemization, and Power Spikes
To maximize the chance of a comeback, you need a clear focus:
- Understand Win Condition: Analyze the game state and determine the narrow path to victory. This understanding dictates itemization and playstyle.
    - If you are the only fed person, play selfishly, farm, and focus on getting as much gold as possible.
    - If a teammate is fed, focus on making their game easy (peeling, engaging, pushing waves for them).
    - If the win condition is side laning pressure, even if you wouldn't normally build an item like Hullbreaker (e.g., Jace), adjust your itemization to support that strategy and create pressure that allows your team numbers advantages elsewhere.
- Hit Power Spikes: It is immensely important to force a fight right as you are hitting a significant power spike (e.g., completing your third item). The moment you hit an item is the largest relative power spike you will achieve. Combine hitting a power spike with an opportunity (like an enemy showing on the other side of the map) to force a successful fight.
Overall, playing from behind is like being an underdog gambler: you can't match your opponent's wealth, so you must rely on carefully chosen high-risk, high-reward plays that maximize temporary numerical superiority, hoping the favored opponent makes a costly mistake
