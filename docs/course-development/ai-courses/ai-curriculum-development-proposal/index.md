These selections are based on the **AI Courses Development** grant document and Sam’s directive to transition from "Software Engineering" to **"System Architecture."**

| Course ID | Proposed Title | Key Focus Areas |

---

### **Detailed Reasoning for Selections**

#### **1. AI 230: Generative AI (The Foundational Bridge)**

* **Reasoning:** While the reused course **DS 479** covers traditional Machine Learning, it does not deep-dive into the Transformer architecture that powers modern AI. This course is the essential bridge.
* **Strategic Fit:** It moves students from "analyzing data" (Data Science) to "generating value" (Generative AI), which is the core shift Sam highlighted for job market readiness.

#### **2. AI 240: AI Agent (The "Core Requirement")**

* **Reasoning:** Sam explicitly stated that at least one course must focus on **Agentic AI or AI Agents**. Using the **Google Agent Development Kit (ADK)** allows students to build autonomous systems rather than just static chat interfaces.
* **Strategic Fit:** This is where the shift to **System Architecture** happens. Students are no longer just writing code; they are designing the "brain" and "memory" of a system that can take independent action.

#### **3. AI 250: Agentic AI (The Competitive Edge)**

* **Reasoning:** This course incorporates the high-level research Sam identified from **Stanford** and **Berkeley**, specifically the **Model Context Protocol (MCP)**. MCP allows different AI agents and tools to speak the same language.
* **Strategic Fit:** This aligns with the **Cascading Model**. By teaching undergraduate students about MCP and Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocols, you are giving them the "Enterprise AI" skills typically reserved for graduate or doctoral levels, making the degree unique in the market.

### **Foundation/Preparatory Courses (Mandatory)**

Sam emphasized that core technical components remain valid and should not be changed at this time. These are reused from the **BS Applied Computer Science** and **Doctor of IT** prep tracks:

* **IS 201: Fundamentals of Computing:** Provides the baseline environment and programming overview needed for all technology degrees.
* **CS 132: Computer Science I:** The standard introduction to Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) and advanced topics like interfaces and generics.
* **CS 340: Operating Systems:** Essential for understanding process and memory management, which Sam listed as a core requirement for students transitioning to graduate-level AI work.

### **Reasoning Summary for the Hybrid Model**

The selection of these specific existing courses follows Sam's three primary strategic requirements:

1. **Operational Cost Reduction:** Reusing **IS 360**, **DS 410**, and **DS 479** allows the department to focus its development budget on the **three new specialized AI courses** (AI 240, 250, and 260) rather than rebuilding foundational data layers.
2. **Job Market Alignment:** By keeping **DS 479** (Machine Learning) and adding **AI 240** (Agents), students gain both traditional ML skills and cutting-edge Agentic AI skills, addressing the team's concern about student employability.
3. **The Cascading Model:** Courses like **IS 360** and **CS 340** appear at all levels—from **BS** to **DIT** prep—ensuring a consistent technical "source of truth" as students progress through CityU's degree ladder.
