# Data Analyst Roadmap

The roadmap for career progression within the Data Analytics job family is structured using a ten-level framework (Levels 10–100), with progression assessed based on growth in three pillars: **Impact, Behaviours, and Skills**. Skills are cumulative, meaning proficiency at previous levels is typically expected in addition to the requirements of the current level.

The primary focus of the Data Analytics job family is delivering impact by **improving the effectiveness of its area and stakeholders** in making informed, data-driven decisions, often utilizing specialized domain knowledge.

### Data Analyst Career Progression Roadmap (Individual Contributor Pathway)

Below details the progression through the initial senior levels of the Individual Contributor (IC) pathway, focusing on the specialized requirements for the Data Analytics job family:

#### Level 10: Associate/Intern
This level typically involves those at the very early stages of their careers, such as students or recent graduates with little professional experience in a Data role. Significant training and support are expected.

| Pillar | Requirements (Data Analytics Specific) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Impact** | Has an impact within a single unit of delivery against clearly scoped tasks, such as building a dashboard with specific tiles. |
| **Skills/Proficiency** | Is able to produce clear, well annotated, charts and summary statistics that can be easily understood and interpreted by others in response to very specific questions. Demonstrates basic knowledge of statistics and can run basic statistical tests. |

#### Level 20: Associate
Individuals at this level are still early in their careers and focus on solving well-defined problems with strong team support, where the key measure of success remains the ability to learn.

| Pillar | Requirements (Data Analytics Specific) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Impact** | Delivers impact within a squad or small domain, solving well-defined problems. |
| **Skills/Proficiency** | Has a basic understanding of diverse data communication techniques, such as chart types and choice of metrics, and their purpose. Is able to produce clear and informative reports about a small business area that colleagues from other disciplines can use and understand. |

#### Level 30
An individual operating at this level possesses solid technical skills and works with relative autonomy on simpler problems within a single squad or small domain.

| Pillar | Requirements (Data Analytics Specific) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Impact** | Delivers impact within a squad or small domain, solving well-defined problems with **relative autonomy**. Demonstrates ownership of smaller problem spaces, consistently delivering successful outcomes. |
| **Skills/Proficiency** | Is capable in descriptive analysis, data visualization, and storytelling to provide clear insights on straightforward problem spaces with little to no dependencies. Able to develop and maintain dashboards, reports, controls, and data processes. **Produces robust, accurate and repeatable analysis, reports, processes and controls**. |

#### Level 40: Senior
At this level, the individual moves beyond clearly defined accountabilities, delivering impact through collaborative efforts on more organizationally or technically complex problems with **minimal oversight**.

| Pillar | Requirements (Data Analytics Specific) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Impact** | Contributes to squad/team goals by applying sound judgement to complex problems (those with no single ‘right’ answer). Takes responsibility for end-to-end delivery of projects. |
| **Skills/Proficiency** | Able to independently scope and formulate analytical solutions to more complex problem spaces, managing dependencies on other areas or trade-offs. **Independently owns the development and maintenance of a broad range of dashboards, reports, controls and data processes**. Develops analysis, reports, processes, and controls to a high standard, ensuring it is scalable, efficient, and well-controlled. |

#### Level 50: Lead Individual Contributor
The Level 50 IC delivers larger or more complex projects, often working through others, and is accountable for the quality of the data solutions. They provide subject-matter leadership.

| Pillar | Requirements (Data Analytics Specific) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Impact** | Brings capability to bear both directly (through individual delivery) and indirectly (though oversight of others). Contributes to collective goals by applying expertise to the delivery of data products that enable business performance. |
| **Skills/Proficiency** | Possesses specialist knowledge (e.g., deep understanding of the dynamics of customer and data products) and is able to influence the strategy of a large area through actionable insights. **Is trusted to own specialized systems end-to-end** (e.g., marketing channels, business critical reporting and monitoring). Consistently produces and maintains high-quality deliverables, setting the **gold-standard for data analytics on cross-collective topics**. |

#### Level 60: Staff
A Level 60 IC has high proficiency in a key Data capability and applies it to critical business challenges, leading efforts on strategically important problems. They are trusted with business-critical projects and products.

| Pillar | Requirements (Data Analytics Specific) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Impact** | Makes a significant contribution to collective goals through the delivery of complex or high-priority data products. Is a role model and leader within Data, contributing to discipline-level strategy. |
| **Skills/Proficiency** | **Is trusted with the most business-critical reports, investor updates, etc.**. Collaborates effectively with leads across multiple business areas to determine opportunities and tradeoffs in advancing on both the areaʼs ambitions and technical debt investments. |

### Core Progression Concepts

This framework guides career conversations and is intended to support human judgment rather than replace it. Key progression themes across all levels in the Data Discipline include:

1.  **Skills and Behaviors as the Route to Impact:** Impact is defined as the result (measurable contribution to success), while skills and behaviors are the route to achieving that impact. Consistently demonstrating growth in all three areas is necessary for progress.
2.  **Focus on Themes over Tasks:** The framework avoids providing exhaustive lists of specific activities (e.g., optimizing SQL queries) and instead focuses on highlighting critical themes, skills, and behaviors required at each level.
3.  **Collaborative Development:** Progressing through the levels is a collaborative process involving the individual, their manager, and others, ensuring alignment with personal aspirations, team needs, and organizational demands.
4.  **Ownership and Quality:** As Data Analysts progress, there is an increasing expectation for ownership of problem spaces, moving towards ensuring their analysis and reports are robust, accurate, and repeatable. At higher levels (L40+), this extends to proactively identifying problems in strategy, product, or platform, and improving data products, leaving things better than they found them.
