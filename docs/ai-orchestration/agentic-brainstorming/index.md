# Lesson: The Multi-Agent Feedback Loop

Brainstorming is no longer just a human-to-human activity. It is a multi-layered process involving humans augmented by their own agents.

### The Layers
1. **Human x AI:** Initial ideation and stress-testing of concepts.
2. **Human x Human (Agent-Assisted):** Collaborating with peers whose agents provide real-time data or counter-arguments.
3. **Agent x Agent:** Handing off a context-rich prompt from one model (Gemini for brainstorming) to another (Claude for HTML refinement).

**The Theory:** *Collaborative Intelligence (CQ).* Innovation now happens at the intersection of diverse perspectives. By using agents during a brainstorm, I am utilizing **The Medici Effect**, exponentially increasing the number of "idea intersections" available in a single session.

### **Theory: Collaborative Intelligence (CQ) & A2A Protocols**
We are moving from "Human-AI" to "Human-Agent-Agent-Human" loops. This is supported by the **Theory of Collective Intelligence**, which suggests that a group's success is determined by the diversity of its members' perspectives. By involving multiple agents (e.g., your Gemini vs. a peer's Claude), you are performing **Multi-Agent Cross-Examination**.

### **Sample Scenario: The "Architectural Vibe-Check"**
You are debating a new app architecture with a friend. You use **Gemini** to brainstorm the creative "user delight" features, while your friend uses **Claude** to stress-test the technical feasibility.
* **The Result:** The agents "argue" through the humans, uncovering a flaw in the integration that neither human would have spotted alone.

### **The Process**
1.  **Define the Goal:** Set a clear objective for the brainstorm.
2.  **Agent Sourcing:** Assign roles to different LLMs (e.g., "Gemini: Optimist/Creative," "Claude: Realist/Critical").
3.  **Synthesis Loop:** Bring the agent outputs back to the human conversation for final decision-making.
4.  **Protocol Sync:** Ensure all agents are working from the same "Context Base" (your Jekyll notes).
